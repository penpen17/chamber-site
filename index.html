A 3D-printed imaging chamber for multi-day live imaging of plant roots

A biocompatible, low-cost chamber that keeps Arabidopsis seedlings growing healthily on vertical- and horizontal-stage confocal microscopes.

    Motivation

    Responses take time

    Plant root responses to abiotic and biotic signals can be time-dependent, so a platform that supports long-term imaging while keeping the samples healthy is essential.

    Roots grow with gravity

    Vertical-stage microscopy lets plants grow naturally along the gravity vector (gravitropism), but compatible sample-mounting methods still need to be developed.

    Commercial options fall short

    Current commercial chambers are basic and expensive. They work well for cell cultures on standard horizontal microscopes, but are limited for live imaging of growing samples.

    Design

    Printing on mesh

    The frame is printed directly onto a nylon mesh, fusing the two together. The mesh keeps the sample moist, and the printed frame supports whole Arabidopsis seedlings and makes them easier to transfer.

    Modular, semi-closed chamber

    The chamber is modular and fits the microscope stage, and its semi-closed design helps maintain sterility during long imaging sessions.

    Vertical and horizontal stages

    The same chamber is used upright on a vertical stage, so roots grow along gravity, or in a stage insert on a standard inverted microscope. Switch between the setups in the 3D viewer above.

    Fabrication

    Printer & material

    Printed on a Bambu Lab A1 mini in PETG Translucent and PETG-HF.

    Coverslip assembly

    Coverslips are bonded with LOCTITE® EA M-31CL, a medical-grade epoxy adhesive tested to ISO 10993 for biocompatibility.

    Printing on mesh

    The nylon mesh is placed on the print bed and the frame is printed over it, so mesh and frame fuse without glue. Open the "Printing on mesh" tab in the 3D viewer to see the setup.

    Results

    Confocal time-lapse imaging of an Arabidopsis root tip in the chamber, tracking the root while bacteria colonize its surface.
